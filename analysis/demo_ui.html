MultiCam
Ask a question about a place filmed by four cameras: a scorer picks the few seconds of video that matter, and an AI model answers from only those. 5 hand-picked questions · picking clips costs time, it does not save it Video: MEVA, CC-BY-4.0
What is this?

Four fixed security cameras film the same place for five minutes each, about 20 minutes of video, and the model can look at only a limited number of frames, so which frames it gets matters. For each question, a small matching model (SigLIP, or ViCLIP) scores short clips from every camera against the question or its answer options, and a vision-language model (InternVL3-8B) answers the multiple-choice question from only the clips it kept.

This page replays recorded runs and computes nothing live. The five questions were hand-picked to show how it works, not drawn at random. Picking clips costs time rather than saving it: choosing the clips takes about 50 seconds per question with SigLIP (about 42 with ViCLIP), and the answer call itself about 3 seconds.

On all 1,033 MEVA questions at the same 96-frame budget, picking clips with SigLIP raises accuracy from 43.85% (every camera) to 49.10% (ViCLIP: 48.31%). No setup that watches the video yet beats 54.70%, the score you get from answer-letter patterns alone.

SigLIP / ViCLIP: models that match pictures or clips to text. InternVL3-8B: the model that watches and answers.
